Literature Survey

Background & Related Work

Clinical Characterization

Stuttering is a speech fluency disorder characterized by involuntary disruptions: sound/syllable repetitions (e.g., "b-b-ball"), prolongations (e.g., "ssssun"), and speech blocks. It affects 5–8% of children and ~1% of the global population, with the critical window being ages 5–10.

Psychosocial Impact: Children experience social anxiety, reduced academic participation, diminished self-esteem, and communication avoidance behaviors.

ML Approaches Reviewed

The field has evolved from traditional classifiers to state-of-the-art transformer models:

  • SVM + MFCC 72–80% accuracy — baseline
  • CNN on Spectrogram 78–82% accuracy
  • Wav2Vec2-Base (English) 84–86% accuracy
  • CNN + ConvLSTM 85–88% accuracy
  • Our Wav2Vec2 XLS-R 300M 88.18% | F1: 0.891

Existing Commercial Apps

Current commercial applications — Stuttering Therapy Lite, Fluency Tracker, DAF Pro, Stamurai — offer isolated features. None combines automated audio-only detection, multilingual support, AI-driven exercise recommendation, and continuous progress tracking within a single integrated platform.

Digital Therapy Research

Castillo et al. demonstrated ASR + fluency tracking can enable semi-autonomous remote therapy. Cloud-based platforms have been advocated for rural access. The Lidcombe Programme highlights continuous measurement and parent involvement for children aged 6–12.

Research Gap

Four Critical Gaps Identified

1

Monolingual Limitation

Current automated systems are trained exclusively on English corpora and cannot generalize to other languages. No validated Sinhala stuttering detection model exists in the literature, representing a major clinical gap for Sri Lanka's 21 million people.

2

Static Point-in-Time Assessment

Existing automated tools provide only static assessments rather than continuous longitudinal monitoring. Clinicians cannot track improvement over time, severely limiting therapeutic effectiveness for children during the critical 5–10 age window.

3

No Personalized Adaptive Intervention

Existing platforms offer one-size-fits-all exercises. There is no system that dynamically adjusts therapy difficulty based on individual performance history — a critical requirement for effective, data-driven speech therapy for children.

4

Inaccessibility in Resource-Limited Settings

Rural communities in Sri Lanka have limited access to Speech-Language Pathologists (SLPs). Existing tools require clinic visits and specialized equipment, making early detection and continuous therapy impossible for the majority of affected children.

Research Problem

The Core Problem Statement

Stuttering disrupts the natural flow and rhythm of speech in children aged 5–10. Limited access to qualified Speech-Language Therapists, especially in rural areas, means children go undiagnosed during their most critical developmental window.

Without early detection: Delayed therapy reduces treatment success, while untreated stuttering leads to anxiety, social withdrawal, and long-term academic disadvantage.
No existing solution addresses Sinhala — Sri Lanka's primary language — leaving a substantial clinical gap for a population of ~21 million.

Methodology

System Architecture & Methodology

Component 1: Stuttering Classifier

Fine-tuned Wav2Vec2 XLS-R 300M on a stratified bilingual corpus (English SEP-28K + Sinhala recordings). Binary classifier: Stutter vs. No-Stutter. 80/20 stratified train-test split on language and label simultaneously to ensure equitable language performance.

Stutter-Type Classifier: Downstream Random Forest on 13-MFCC features achieves 98.34% for 4 classes: Part-word Repetition, Prolongation, Block, Whole-word Repetition.

Component 2: Exercise Recommender

Hybrid rule-based + ML architecture classifies users into mild, moderate, and severe stuttering levels. Learns from historical accuracy, attempts, and response time to dynamically adjust exercise difficulty. Continuously updates cloud-based recommendations to simulate clinician-guided therapy.

K-Means pseudo-labeling followed by supervised KNN classification — effective for constructing classifiers where per-recording expert annotations are unavailable at scale.

Component 3: Pronunciation Checker

Dual-fallback ASR: OpenAI Whisper (primary) + Google Cloud STT (en-US & si-LK) for robust recognition. IPA phoneme sequence aligned using Levenshtein edit distance and phonetically-informed substitution costs. Composite score: 50% phoneme edit distance + 25% speech rate + 25% fluency continuity.

SVM fluency classifier: ~420-dim acoustic feature vector (MFCCs, F0/pitch, ZCR, RMS, spectral features). RBF kernel, C=10, gamma=0.01 — 89.75% accuracy.

Component 4: Progress Dashboard

LSTM architecture captures temporal patterns across multiple therapy sessions. Provides quantitative severity scores and confidence levels. Deployed as a RESTful Flask service for real-time prediction delivery to Flutter mobile app. Progress visualized through intuitive dashboards with trend lines.

Early stopping with patience=7 and best-checkpoint saving mitigates overfitting during fine-tuning on small task-specific datasets.
